☆ SwapBot: Tool-Centric Robot Manipulation via Interchangeable End-Effectors and Tactile Sensing
Yunchu Zhang, Ray Song, Junhan Jiao, Liyiming Ke, Yuchen Yang, Ruihan Gao, Emma Romig, Siddhartha Srinivasa, Yiyue Luo
Robot manipulation in everyday environments inherently involves substantial tool usage. Seemingly simple household tasks, such as cooking pan-fried tofu, require forceful cutting, delicate brushing, and precise placement using different tools under contact-rich interaction. Thus, the ability to autonomously swap and use task-appropriate tools is important for tool-centric manipulation. We introduce SwapBot, a modular, tool-centric end effector system featuring low-cost adapters that quickly convert common household tools into robot end effectors. Rather than pursuing a single universal gripper, SwapBot adopts a unified design principle that combines a fixed robot-side coupling interface with low-cost, tool-side adapters, enabling compatibility with diverse tools, including articulated ones, and multiple robot platforms. Each adapter integrates compact tactile sensors, supports fast autonomous swapping, and interfaces with an intuitive teleoperation system for efficient demonstration collection. User studies show that, during teleoperation, SwapBot reduces task completion time and operator workload. Using collected demonstrations on the robot, we train imitation learning policies to successfully perform tool-centric tasks like cooking tofu, stirring salad, flipping meat. Our ablation study shows that using tactile-augmented demonstrations improves policy performance across contact-rich skills, increasing success rates by 15 to 25 percentage points over RGB-only policies. These results highlight modular, tactile-equipped tools as a practical and extensible pathway toward robust and capable robot manipulation. Project website https://personalrobotics.github.io/SwapBot.
☆ SymAnt: Grounding Long-Horizon Action Anticipation with Adaptable Symbolic Priors WACV 2027
Fnu Aryan, Yaqi Xie, Joseph Campbell, Woojun Kim, Kwonjoon Lee, Hossein Nourkhiz Mahjoub, Ehsan Moradi-Pari, Katia Sycara, Simon Stepputtis
As robots and intelligent systems increasingly interact with humans, they must anticipate long sequences of future actions from only brief observations. While purely neural models typically require extensive observations to predict future actions and remain difficult to interpret and adjust, symbolic priors offer a structured alternative. To this end, we introduce SymAnt, a novel neuro-symbolic approach that unifies the expressiveness of neural networks with the structured reasoning of scene and knowledge graphs. Given a short video, SymAnt performs a joint-search over both graphs to construct a symbolic context, enabling unified reasoning over spatial relationships, object attributes, affordances, and associated actions. This symbolic context serves as a prior for an iterative refinement process that progressively generates and improves the sequence of future actions. By grounding predictions in symbolic context, SymAnt anticipates long-horizon actions from short observation windows in a parameter-efficient manner, while enabling users to directly edit the underlying knowledge to adjust predictions without further training. Through our experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness of SymAnt, particularly when only short observation contexts are available, across five diverse datasets: Breakfast, 50 Salads, EPIC-KITCHENS, EGTEA Gaze+, and Assembly101.

☆ A Conveyor Belt Mechanism for Repositionable Magnetic Coupling Between Continuum Robots
Ella Walsh, Eric Diller, Jessica Burgner-Kahrs
This work proposes a novel conveyor belt mechanism enabling continuous, active repositioning of functional elements along the body of a tendon-driven continuum robot. Up to three conveyor belt mechanisms are integrated within a 9 mm diameter continuum robot. We demonstrate equivalent or reduced conveyor belt actuation forces compared to robot tendon actuation forces. As a case study, we mount permanent magnets along two conveyor belts, and demonstrate actively reconfigurable inter-robot coupling. Coupling point location and quantity can be adjusted during robot operation to actively tune the effective stiffness of the coupled multi-robot system. We show that effective stiffness follows a predictable inverse-cubic relationship with coupling location, and increases with the number of coupling points. These findings demonstrate that the conveyor belt mechanism enables active functional element repositioning for changing tasks requirements, with magnetic inter-robot coupling as one proof-of-concept.

☆ Gym-Anything for Robotics: Scaling physical simulations with digital agents
Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
Realistic, fast robotics simulations could enable large-scale training of embodied AI models that transfer to the real world. However, despite decades of progress, available simulations cover only a narrow range of real-world tasks and physical phenomena. The core bottleneck is that creating better simulations requires significant expert effort and therefore does not scale. To address this, we introduce Gym-Anything for Robotics, a framework in which coding agents create realistic robotics simulations. Our core insight is that simulation creation is a coding agent task, so improvements in coding agents translate directly into richer and more faithful simulations. We select simulations based on U.S. GDP data to cover economically valuable physical work, define what makes a good simulation and a good task, and develop an adversarial creation-audit loop that checks each simulation and task against these definitions. Using this pipeline, we build RoboWorld, a set of 25 simulations with 25 test and 175 training RL environments of varying difficulty. Even the best frontier models fail on every RoboWorld test task, achieving an average reward of only 0.06. Building on RoboWorld, we introduce several new ways for frontier models to improve, including in-context learning across repeated attempts, a privileged teacher that reads the simulator state and teaches a student in context, self-assisted learning from the model's own privileged experience, and code policies that generate demonstrations across varied scenes to train vision-language-action models (VLAs) such as $π_{0.5}$. Each of these substantially improves performance. We release our pipeline, RL environments, and benchmark to facilitate future research. Code and data are available at https://gym-anything.github.io/robotics
☆ A Tutorial on Shape Servoing of Deformable Objects-Part I: Fundamentals
Ignacio Cuiral-Zueco, Alessio Caporali, Miguel Aranda, Gianluca Palli, Gonzalo López-Nicolás, Youcef Mezouar
In robotics, shape control (or shape servoing) refers to the automatic manipulation of a deformable object to achieve a desired shape. It involves a feedback loop that exploits the relation between robot motion and changes in the measured shape. This relation, known as the interaction Jacobian or, in this context, the deformation Jacobian, depends on the object's mechanical properties, geometry, and boundary conditions (e.g., where the object is grasped). Because these factors are often difficult to model accurately, the deformation Jacobian can be estimated locally from observed shape changes produced by commanded robot motions, without requiring a fine-tuned mechanical model. This article is the first of a two-part tutorial on data-driven Jacobian-based shape control of deformable objects. It introduces the central elements of the approach: the formulation of the shape-control problem, shape representation, estimation and online update of the deformation Jacobian, and a standard control law from the literature. It then assembles these elements into a complete shape-servoing loop, derives a local stability condition for this loop, and illustrates why some target shapes cannot be reached. Part II covers more advanced topics. Throughout both parts, a simulated planar example in which two robot arms manipulate an elastic rod observed by a camera illustrates the concepts.

☆ PhysVLMM: Interaction-Conditioned Physical Belief Maps for Articulated Objects in Robot Scene Maps
Dibyendu Ghosh
Mobile robots must open doors and drawers, yet their scene maps record what each object is and whether it has moved, not how it will respond to a pull. This paper proposes PhysVLMM, a method that maintains a physical belief for every articulated part of a Vision-Language-Motion Map and answers a planner's queries about it, with two algorithms: a map update and a query. Watching a part move provably cannot identify its dynamics, and a measured pull identifies only the pulled part, up to one direction. PhysVLMM therefore (i) keeps that direction as a posterior instead of a point estimate, (ii) passes the evidence of each pull in closed form to the untouched look-alike parts of the map, and (iii) answers whether an action will succeed at a stated risk, or abstains. On real doors and drawers in 18 homes, it identifies the joint type in 86% of held-out pulls (48% for a learned single-view method) and predicts the response to a pull with 41% lower error than differentiable simulation (CRPS 0.323 vs. 0.544), four orders of magnitude faster (13 ms vs. 230 s per case). The posterior lowers the final-position error from 0.460 to 0.270, sharing lowers the force error of untouched parts from 56% to 42%, and at every stated risk from 0.02 to 0.30 the false-safe answers stay within it. Pooling all measured parts of a home does as well as the look-alike groups, and simpler variants predict where a pulled part stops at least as well.
☆ DAWN: Diffusion-based Alignment and unfastening Workflow Network
Hemant Kumar, Piyush Goenka, Nikhil Chopra
We present DAWN (Diffusion-based Alignment and unfastening Workflow Network), a hierarchical robotic framework designed to perform real-world, unfastening tasks through multi-modal sensory integration. Autonomous robotic unfastening is crucial for improving recycling processes, enhancing safety, and reducing operational costs; yet, it remains predominantly manual due to inherent challenges in precise screwdriver engagement with fasteners, detailed force execution, and robustness to environmental variability. To address the complexity of learning long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks, we subdivide the unfastening task procedure into two sequential stages: (1) alignment & insertion, and (2) unfastening. DAWN consists of three key components: AlignNet, a diffusion-based policy ensuring precise screwdriver tip engagement; LatchNet, a multi-modal classifier reliably detecting successful stage transitions; and UnfastNet, which achieves stable, force-controlled screw removal. Experimental validation on a Franka Emika robot demonstrates that DAWN achieves alignment & insertion success rates comparable to trained human operators, robustly transitions between stages, and attains an impressive success rate in unfastening across various unseen scenarios. Ablation studies further elucidate critical input modalities and effective training strategies, highlighting DAWN's scalability, robustness, and data efficiency.

Xiangyu Xu, Bangshuai Peng, Ziyi Liu, Mengjie Zhao, Chenhao Lu, Zhengyang Fan, Huazhe Xu, Kaizhe Hu

☆ A Two-Wheeled Quadrotor with All-Clockwise Tilted Rotors for Level-Attitude Surface Locomotnion
Takumi Ito, Satoshi Nakano
Conventional wheel-equipped quadrotor UAVs must tilt their bodies to generate lateral forces, limiting their ability to maintain a desired attitude during lateral motion. This paper proposes a wheeled quadrotor with all-clockwise tilted rotors that uses surface contact as a constraint to generate propulsion along the contact surface without tilting the vehicle. We analyze feasible forces and system controllability under surface contact and optimize the vehicle design considering different rotor-spin configurations. The optimization results show that a configuration in which all four rotors spin clockwise can generate a larger tangential force than the conventional alternating-spin configuration. This approach enables both aerial and contact-based locomotion without additional rotor-tilting actuators or mechanisms. Simulation results demonstrate both hovering in free space and horizontal locomotion on a floor while maintaining a level attitude.
☆ Fundamental Limits of Sampling-Based Reachability: Geometry and Flow Distortion CoRL 2026
Jixian Liu, Ihab Tabbara, Hussein Sibai, Enrique Mallada
Sampling-based reachability estimates reachable sets from finitely many simulated trajectory endpoints. Existing guarantees often control missed probability mass or approximation error relative to the convex hull of the reachable set, leaving the geometric error relative to the reachable set incompletely characterized. We study this error as a problem of support estimation under flow distortion. Our assumptions combine a uniform lower bound on the local volume density of the initial set, a lower bound on the sampling density, and a one-sided Lipschitz constant $μ$ for the vector field. These conditions admit initial sets with corners and dynamics that may expand or contract. In the fine-accuracy regime, $\tilde{\mathcal O}\bigl(2^ne^{nμT}(R/r)^n/(ρκ)\bigr)$ independent endpoint samples suffice for Hausdorff accuracy $r$ using the endpoint cloud. Here, $n$ is the state dimension, $T$ is the horizon, $R$ is the volume-equivalent radius, and $ρ$ and $κ$ quantify sampling density and local volume density. For $n\ge2$ and sufficiently small $κ$, a minimax lower bound over all estimators based only on independent endpoints matches these dependencies up to a dimension-dependent factor $2\cdot4^n$ and logarithmic factors, within the stated parameter range and when the state domain accommodates the construction. The exponential burden in dimension, and in the horizon for expanding systems, is therefore intrinsic to this observation model. Planar experiments illustrate the predicted effects of corners and contraction; robot-arm experiments show how empirical accuracy deteriorates with dimension.

☆ Adaptive search with path-constrained mobile sensors using multi-arm bandits
Abraham P. Vinod, Parth K. Thaker, Gautam Dasarathy, Stefano Di Cairano
We study a constrained sequential decision problem for detecting regions of interest from noisy online measurements collected by a team of energy-constrained mobile sensors. The sensor motion is restricted to a precomputed set of feasible paths that satisfy mobility constraints, limited available energy by requiring return to charging stations, and avoidance of known obstacles. We cast the resulting active path selection as a multi-armed bandit problem and propose an iterative search algorithm that adaptively assigns paths using confidence bounds computed from the collected data. We also propose a graph-based method to construct low-cardinality path covers under the team's constraints. We establish finite-time and anytime probabilistic correctness guarantees for the proposed algorithm, derive non-asymptotic upper bounds on the times required to detect all regions of interest and to label all nodes, and show that these bounds match algorithm-independent lower bounds up to constant factors for a broad class of problem instances. Using numerical simulations, we illustrate the behavior of the proposed approach and the effect of path redundancy and team size on search performance.

☆ Improving Fine-Grained Manipulation through Demonstration Expansion and Execution Design CoRL 2026
Jingxu Gu, Dongze Lian
Fine-grained manipulation requires a robot policy to follow instructions, remain robust to visual variations, and translate predicted actions into precise robot control. We present Demonstration Expansion and Execution Design (DEED), a framework for adapting a pretrained vision-language-action policy to the five MetaFine tasks through structured demonstration expansion and execution design. DEED generates demonstrations for different instructions from the same initial scene and renders each physical trajectory under different camera positions and lighting conditions while preserving its original states and action targets. ☆ Optimal Path Planning with Localization constraints
Chinmay Mundane, Manav Mishra, PB Sujit
This paper studies UAV path planning in areas where GPS signals are not present. Because these drones can easily get lost when they don't have GPS, they must rely on dead reckoning and occasionally pass near cellular towers, which act as landmarks to reset and fix their position. The goal is to find the shortest path from a start to a goal location while keeping the estimation uncertainty within a given bound. To solve this problem, we propose a localization-constrained A* planner that treats uncertainty as a hard constraint during search. The robot state and covariance are propagated using an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), and only paths that satisfy the covariance limit are considered. We also show that the proposed method does not affect heuristic used in A* and remains admissible and consistent, ensuring optimality among all feasible paths. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations and PyBullet quadrotor test show that the proposed method finds shorter and more reliable paths compared to RRT* and PRM, while always satisfying the uncertainty constraint.
☆ socx: GPU-Accelerated Chance-Constrained Stochastic Optimal Control using JAX
Tanmay Dokania, Yashwanth Kumar Nakka
In this article, we present socx, a GPU-accelerated solver for lifted convex reformulations of linear stochastic optimal control under chance constraints, implemented in JAX. We demonstrate that by exploiting the sparse and independent structure of the problem, distributed optimization can reduce it to simpler problems: independent linear-quadratic tracking (LQT) problems and Euclidean projections onto sets. These simpler problems can be solved in parallel and then coupled using the constraints. To validate our solver, we run it on a hover-linearized 3D quadrotor and show a speed-up of up to $98\times$ over MOSEK and up to $2224\times$ over SCS. socx also solves horizons up to $N=1120$, whereas MOSEK runs out of memory at $N=320$. We present ablations to isolate the key components that enable the speed-up.
☆ Continuous-Action World Models: Learning Cross-Embodiment Spatiotemporal Dynamics Priors
Liangyu Li, Qingwen Liu
We introduce Continuous-Action World Models (CoAWM), a framework for learning cross-embodiment spatiotemporal dynamics priors that support robot action generation across embodiments and execution frequencies. At its core is a shared latent function space that captures the spatial and temporal structure of robot actions. We learn this space through two stages. First, function pretraining brings trajectories from different embodiments and sampling frequencies into a common representation, learning shared dynamics priors through action reconstruction and consistency across temporal sampling views. By accommodating heterogeneous robot trajectories, this stage broadens the range of data sources available for pretraining and facilitates scaling to larger and more diverse datasets. Second, we connect the pretrained function space to a world-model backbone, enabling observations, language instructions, and the current robot state to condition the prediction of a latent action function. A single decoder takes any query time and returns the corresponding end-effector action. By separating shared dynamics priors from task-conditioned action selection and temporal sampling, our approach provides a unified foundation for transferring robot experience across embodiments and control frequencies.

Behnam Moradi

We present RPO-PDT, an embodied conversational agent that adapts its conversational role to the current participant. It acts as a Per- sonal Development Tutor (PDT) when supporting students and as a simulated student when faculty rehearse tutoring strategies. Voluntary closed-set participant verification informs a confidence- controlled role manager: stable enrolled-faculty matches activate the Student role, while enrolled students and unmatched partici- pants use the default PDT role and uncertain observations cause no transition. The demon- stration exposes both role-switching behaviour and these privacy controls in interaction.

♻ ☆ In-Context Learning World Models for Robot Dual-Arm Manipulation of Soft Garment with Partial Observations
Hengyi Sim, Quentin Rouxel, Dennis Hong, Darwin Caldwell, Fei Chen
Robust manipulation of soft objects, such as garments, is a critical skill for service and household robots to adopt, but remains challenging due to its high-dimensional state space, and material-dependent deformations. Humans address this challenge by using mental world models that "imagine" the shape and material properties from partial observations, which enables them to predict how actions affect future states. As such, embedding such world models in robots allows for better generalization in manipulating a wide variety of garments in the real world. However, learned world models typically require large amounts of real world data that is strenuous to collect, and a mechanism to infer latent properties that govern dynamics. We present a Soft-Object World Model that performs generative full-state estimation and action-conditioned dynamics prediction using a transformer backbone trained on large-scale simulated soft object interactions, with measures to mitigate the sim-to-real gap. By learning to infer latent shape and material cues through in-context learning over a variable-length partial point-cloud history, the model generalizes across robot embodiments, material parameters, garment types and shapes, and manipulation coordination modes without weight updates. The world model is then integrated with a Goal-Conditioned action planner for motion synthesis. We validate the approach in simulation and real world for bimanual garment folding, reporting improvements in reconstruction fidelity, prediction accuracy, and task success. As egocentric video becomes increasingly available for embodied learning, an important research question is how to turn this low-cost, scalable source of data into robot manipulation capabilities. Egocentric videos naturally provide rich context for high-level task understanding, while real-robot demonstrations with wrist observations and action annotations provide supervision for fine-grained action alignment. We present Ego-CEVR, a data-native framework for hierarchical visual representation learning that assigns distinct learning objectives to global and interaction representations. Ego-CEVR supports independent pretraining of its global video module on large-scale human egocentric videos and videos from diverse robot embodiments, without requiring robot action labels or paired wrist observations.
♻ ☆ Whole-Body Motion Guided Reference Generation for Uncertainty-Aware Compliant Assistance
Muyuan Ma, Long Cheng, Lijun Han, Haotian Zhai, Xinpan Meng, Xiuze Xia, Houcheng Li, Zeyu Liu
Exoskeletons have shown great potential for augmenting human physical capabilities, yet generating reliable assistance remains challenging because human motion patterns and interaction requirements vary substantially in practice. To address this problem, a dual-loop assistance strategy is proposed to couple multimodal exoskeleton trajectory generation with variable admittance control, where trajectory variance links the outer-loop generator and the inner-loop controller. The generator synthesizes future exoskeleton trajectories through denoising, primarily using whole-body human motion and exoskeleton proprioception, with task descriptions as auxiliary semantic cues. To reduce cross-modal representation mismatch, a three-stage training pipeline is introduced to progressively optimize the human motion encoder, the exoskeleton proprioception encoder, and the trajectory generator. In the inner loop, multiple future trajectories are sampled from the generator to estimate the trajectory mean and variance. The mean is used as the control reference, while the variance provides an uncertainty measure for prediction reliability. This measure is further incorporated into a stiffness regulation law, enabling compliant assistance with input-to-state stability under bounded lumped interaction input. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly reduces muscle activation levels compared with standard baselines, while also decreasing users' metabolic energy expenditure relative to the unassisted condition.

♻ ☆ NTR: Neural Token Reconstruction for Scene Token Bottleneck in End-to-End Driving
Jiahui Li, Jiawei Sun, Zixiang Ren, Ming Liu, Jiamin Shi, Ruiteng Zhao, Zhiyang Liu, Liying Liu, Zuoguan Wang, Kaidi Yang
Recent perception-free end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving methods bypass explicit perception outputs by compressing dense image patch tokens into compact scene tokens for downstream trajectory generation and scoring. While these scene tokens form a compact visual bottleneck for the planner, they receive supervision solely from the planning objective, providing limited constraints on the encoded visual information. To address this limitation, we introduce Neural Token Reconstruction (NTR), a representation learning framework to directly constrain the compact scene-token bottleneck in perception-free driving. NTR introduces a self-distillation masked latent reconstruction objective that reconstructs masked patch-level latent features using only compact scene tokens as reconstruction memory. This forces reconstruction gradients to pass exclusively through the scene-token bottleneck, encouraging scene tokens to preserve richer and less redundant visual representations for planning. We further introduce semantic priors derived from foundation-model annotations as a weak semantic interface biasing reconstruction targets toward driving-related structures without introducing explicit perception heads. All auxiliary reconstruction components are removed at inference time, leaving the deployed planner unchanged. NTR achieves state-of-the-art performance on three public autonomous driving benchmarks, including 8.0461 RFS on Waymo E2E and 94.1 PDMS / 90.9 EPDMS on NavSim1&2. The learned scene tokens exhibit lower pairwise redundancy and higher effective rank, indicating that effective bottleneck supervision improves both compact visual representation learning and planning performance.

I-Tak Ieong, Hao Tang

♻ ☆ ActionUNet: Improving Robustness of VLA Models with Efficient Multi-scale Fine-tuning
Di Zhu, Ziheng Yan, Fang Wan
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown great promise for robotic manipulation by mapping multi-modal semantics to physical actions. However, this mapping inherently struggles to align these coarse-grained semantics with fine-grained temporal execution. It leaves VLA models with limited generalization and insufficient robustness in cluttered environments. To overcome this issue, we propose ActionUNet, an efficient multi-scale fine-tuning framework that enhances pre-trained VLA models with minimal computational cost. ActionUNet first constructs a lightweight temporal U-Net within the temporal-aligned action feature space to fuse hierarchical structural priors, effectively bridging the scale gap between semantics and temporal executions. Recognizing that multi-scale modeling can disrupt microscopic temporal continuity and cause mechanical oscillations, ActionUNet then employs a conditional SIREN as a continuous action decoder. Equipped with explicit second-order smoothness constraints, this decoder guarantees temporal continuity and reduces high-frequency motion jitter. By smoothing temporal discontinuities from multi-scale fusion, this continuous formulation reduces mechanical execution failures while preserving the base VLA model's generalization and manipulation robustness. Extensive experiments on RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO-Plus benchmarks, together with real-world hard evaluations, demonstrate that ActionUNet significantly improves π0.5 success rates by absolute 9.8%, 6.1%, and 11.4%, respectively, while also generalizing to the regression-based OpenVLA-OFT backbone, highlighting its effectiveness and efficiency as a fine-tuning strategy. ♻ ☆ Multimodal Progress-Residual Fusion for Anticipating Navigation Retreat in Robotic Flexible Endoscopy
Chi Kit Ng, Tianchun Wu, Jiatan Sun, Yuming Sun, Jianru Zhang, Hongliang Ren
Flexible robotic endoscopes provide access through tortuous anatomy, where friction, deformation, and wall interaction separate commanded insertion from useful distal progress. Operators interpret this mismatch and retreat to recover a navigable configuration. Recognizing the approach to that decision requires relating motor intent to distal response and the local visual scene. We introduce Multimodal Progress-Residual Fusion (MPRF), a causal model that anticipates operator retreat from commands, electromagnetic (EM) tracking, and endoscopic video. A nominal-progress model predicts the response expected in normal or successful navigation; a temporal predictor combines its EM residual with all three observed modalities. In a 150-episode colon-phantom collection, MPRF achieves 0.580 +/- 0.007 AUPRC across three seeds, compared with 0.511 for the strongest reduced-modality control. Complete multimodal histories provide the principal ranking advantage, while the residual adds an explicit intent-response representation with a small, uncertain increment over raw fusion. Analysis of frozen alarms relates warning timing to the behavioral label and explains the increase in event recall under a longer matching window. These results establish a basis for behavioral assistance during operator-controlled navigation.
♻ ☆ Mixture of Frames Policy: Multi-Frame Action Denoising for Bimanual Mobile Manipulation
Dian Wang, Jisang Park, Xiaomeng Xu, Han Zhang, Shuran Song, Jeannette Bohg
Robotic manipulation is inherently multi-frame: local actions may be simple in an end-effector frame, while transport, upright-object handling, and whole-body coordination are better represented in a base-aligned frame. However, modern diffusion-based visuomotor policies typically commit to a single predefined action frame, forcing one denoiser to model action distributions that are often unnecessarily complex in that frame. We propose Mixture of Frames Policy (MoF), a diffusion policy that performs synchronized action denoising across multiple coordinate frames. MoF maintains a single canonical diffusion state, re-expresses it in several task-relevant frames, applies frame-specialized denoisers, and fuses their noise predictions back in the canonical frame. To make this possible for intermediate noisy diffusion states, we adopt a column-based 6D rotation representation within an SE(3) action parameterization that supports exact, differentiable frame transformations without requiring noisy rotations to lie on the SO(3) manifold. Across nine simulated bimanual manipulation tasks, we show that the best action frame is task-dependent and that MoF improves over oracle frame selection and standard Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) baselines. We further evaluate MoF on two real-world bimanual mobile manipulation tasks, demonstrating that it outperforms all constituent single-frame baselines.
♻ ☆ From Video to Vision: Learning Generalizable Humanoid Carrying from Human Motion
Jianming Lu, Ziluo Ding, Zhen Zhao, Shuyang Jin, Han Wen, Xinrun Xu, Zhiyuan Xu
Most existing approaches rely on video or motion-capture data for reinforcement learning (RL) training; however, all of these methods require an upper-level module to generate reference trajectories in real time. In object carrying tasks, the humanoid can only lift objects from designated locations along the reference trajectory, resulting in poor spatial generalization. Moreover, existing policies can only be evaluated inside a motion-capture studio. To address these limitations, we propose \textbf{MOVER}, a multi-stage, Teacher-Assistant-Student, RL training framework for humanoid object carrying. We first collect data from videos and employ GENMO and GMR to retarget the video data, combining this with data augmentation techniques to generalize the data across objects of varying shapes and sizes. In the \textbf{first stage}, building upon the DeepMimic \cite{peng2018deepmimic} motion-training framework, we train the humanoid to transport objects from specified locations along designated trajectories. In the \textbf{second stage}, we relax the tracking of the reference motion and adjust the reward so that the humanoid learns to transport objects from different positions. In the \textbf{third stage}, we apply a Dagger-PPO distillation method to distill away the proprioceptive position observations and reference joint position of the humanoid. This allows the deployed policy to operate without relying on global position information from a motion-capture system; instead, the humanoid can directly perceive the object's pose from its onboard RGB-D camera to complete the carrying task. We design a series of simulation experiments to compare the success rates across stages (Stages 1 vs. 2) and before versus after distillation. We further transfer the policy to a real humanoid, successfully transporting objects at varying positions in a motion-capture-free setting.
